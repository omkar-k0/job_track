# Early-career and German-language interview prep

Read when the role is a Werkstudent, internship or thesis role, the posting is junior or entry level, or the interview is in German. Nothing here is a claim about the user: every story, number and tool still comes from `stories`, `profile` or `reusable-answers`.

## Story sources when there is little work history

Fill the STAR slots from these, in the user's words:
- A course or group project: the goal, the user's part (say "I" for their own part), the tools, what came out of it (a grade, a demo, a metric, a repository, feedback).
- A thesis or seminar paper: the question, the method, the data, one result, one thing that went wrong and what changed.
- A hackathon, open-source contribution, student initiative, tutoring, or a research assistant (HiWi) job.
- A Werkstudent job or an earlier internship, even a short one.
- Non-work stories count if they are real: organising an event, a team sport captaincy, a part-time job. Use them for teamwork, reliability and handling pressure.
Strong early-career answers say what the user decided, what they would do differently, and how they know it worked. A weak spot is not covered up: it is answered honestly when asked, and it stays out of unprompted statements.

If `stories` has fewer than four usable stories, offer once to help build one from a project in `profile`: ask what the goal was, what the user did, what happened, how they know. Show the draft and ask the user to confirm or correct every line before it is saved.

## Typical Werkstudent and internship questions (English / German)

Build the sheet from the ones that fit the posting; add the user's story or an honest thin-spot note for each.

- Tell us about yourself. / Stellen Sie sich bitte kurz vor.
- Why this company, why this role? / Warum bewerben Sie sich bei uns und auf diese Stelle?
- Why a working-student role (or internship) and not a full-time job? / Warum eine Werkstudentenstelle?
- How many hours can you work, and how does that fit your timetable and exam periods? / Wie viele Stunden pro Woche können Sie arbeiten, und wie passt das zu Vorlesungen und Klausurphasen?
- When can you start, and for how long? / Ab wann stehen Sie zur Verfügung, für welchen Zeitraum?
- Where are you in your studies? / In welchem Semester sind Sie, wann endet das Studium, planen Sie eine Abschlussarbeit?
- Which projects or courses are most relevant to this role? / Welche Projekte oder Kurse sind für die Stelle am relevantesten?
- Walk us through a project end to end. / Erzählen Sie von einem Projekt von der Idee bis zum Ergebnis.
- Which tools do you use, and how did you learn them? / Mit welchen Tools arbeiten Sie, wie haben Sie sie gelernt?
- Tell us about a mistake and what you did. / Erzählen Sie von einem Fehler und wie Sie damit umgegangen sind.
- Working in a team, a disagreement. / Zusammenarbeit im Team, ein Konflikt.
- How do you organise your work next to your studies? / Wie organisieren Sie Arbeit und Studium?
- What do you expect to learn here? / Was möchten Sie bei uns lernen?
- Pay expectation. / Gehaltsvorstellung. Answer from `reusable-answers`, in the unit the interviewer uses (per hour or per month, gross). If the user has no figure, say so in Thin spots and let them decide; do not invent one.
- Do you have questions for us? / Haben Sie Fragen an uns?

Technical rounds for a data or applied AI role: take the topics from the posting's requirements (for example SQL joins and aggregation, Python data handling, a model evaluation question, train and test splits, explaining a result to a non-expert) and tie each to a real project of the user. Offer a short practice outline, never an invented implementation.

## Honest answers on the sensitive ones

- Hours and the 20-hour reference: if asked how many hours the user can work, the answer is the user's own `weekly_hours`. If the employer asks for more than a student can usually work during lectures, that is a "Confirm with the employer and your university" item on the sheet, not something to answer on the spot.
- Work permit or residence status: the user answers in their own words. The sheet lists "Confirm with your Ausländerbehörde / university how many days you may work" for non-EU students, with no statement of the rule.
- Salary: the sheet shows the user's own floor and the unit; it never says what the market pays.
- Gaps (a semester abroad, a changed degree, a break): answered truthfully and briefly when asked.

## German-language interviews

- Check the user's level in `filters.languages`. B2 or above: write the answers in German. Below B2: write short, plain sentences, add a vocabulary list of the technical terms from the posting, and tell the user which questions to rehearse aloud first.
- Translate the user's stories, do not embellish them. Keep their voice: if they write plainly, the German is plain. Flag every idiom you are not sure of for the user to check.
- Register: Sie unless the interviewer offers du. Many startups use du in the posting and in the first mails; follow the interviewer once they use it.
- Useful frames (adapt, do not memorise): "Ich studiere im N. Semester ... an der ...", "In meinem Projekt habe ich ... gemacht", "Mein Anteil war ...", "Das Ergebnis war ...", "Das würde ich heute anders machen: ...", "Dazu habe ich noch keine Erfahrung, ich kenne aber ..." (only when asked directly, with a true "aber").
- The common German flow: Vorstellungsrunde, Fragen zum Lebenslauf, Fachfragen, Fragen des Bewerbers, Eintrittstermin und Gehalt. Some employers run an Assessment Center or a take-home task: ask for the format in the booking mail.
